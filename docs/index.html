Walk-in check-in for barber shops

Customers put their name in on a tablet by the door. The queue shows on the TV. Barbers call the next person up from their phone. It replaces the paper sign-in sheet, and it's free.

What's in it

Four screens, all showing the same live queue. Nobody has to refresh anything.

The kiosk

A tablet by the door. Customers type their name, pick a barber if they want one, and tap Check In. They're told their place in line.

A parent can check their kids in at the same time, up to six people at once. When the shop is closed, the kiosk says so, and when it opens again if you've set opening hours.

Setting up the kiosk

The TV

Who's in the chair and who's waiting, in order. People can see where they are without asking.

The whole room can read it, so names are cut down to a first name and an initial. Barbers see full names on their own screens.

The barbers' screen

Each barber logs in on their own phone, or on a shared tablet. They call the next customer, mark them done, or take a no-show off the list.

Tapped the wrong one? There's an undo for ten minutes. A barber can go on break so customers can't pick them, close the shop to new check-ins, or put up a notice like "Card machine down, cash only today".

The guide for barbers

The owner's side

Add your shops and barbers, set opening hours so the kiosk opens and closes itself, and see which screens are switched on right now.

Charts show cuts per day and per barber, average waits, and the busiest hours of the week. Every action is logged, so you can see who did what and when.

Changing the name, colours and logo

What you need

Next Up runs on a computer you control and is reached through a web browser. There's no app to install from a store.

Somewhere to run it

A small rented server (a VPS) is the usual choice, so barbers can reach it from anywhere. A spare computer in the shop also works. Either way it needs Docker.

A tablet

Any tablet with an up-to-date browser, an iPad or an Android one, mounted by the door.

A TV (optional)

Anything that can show a web page full screen: a smart TV browser, a streaming stick or a small PC.

Phones

Barbers use the phones they already have. A shared tablet behind the counter works too.
